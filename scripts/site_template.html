Retail Cancellation Leakage Analysis

How much sales value does an online retailer lose to cancellations, and how much of it can it do something about? An SQL and Python analysis of 1.07 million rows of real sales from a UK online gift retailer (UCI Online Retail II, Dec 2009 to Dec 2011).

What the data shows

  • Two huge orders, each placed and cancelled within minutes, cause the two biggest monthly spikes. Leaving out reversals (same customer, product and quantity, cancelled within 24 hours) turns the $headline rate into $adjusted.
  • A few products account for most cancellations: of the $skus products with cancellations other than reversals, the top 5% hold $top5 of that cancelled value.
  • Spain's $spain_rate rate comes down to one customer ($spain_top_share of its cancelled value); without that customer it is $spain_without.
  • For customers with a recorded customer ID the rate is $ci_text, from a bootstrap that resamples whole customers.

Method and limits

The raw data is loaded unchanged and cleaned in SQL with documented rules: duplicate rows, zero prices and non-product codes such as postage are removed. A cancellation counts as a same-day reversal when the same customer cancels the same product and quantity within 24 hours of buying it. The site is rebuilt from the original data and published only after all automated tests pass. Limits: the data gives no reasons for cancelling, the reversal rule is a rule of thumb, and the figures are invoice prices, not profit.
